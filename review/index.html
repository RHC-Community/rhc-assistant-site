Test plan for Zoom Marketplace review

Meeting Assistant joins a Zoom meeting as a participant, through the Meeting SDK, to show slides, run a shared timer and post announcements, controlled from the meeting chat. In a meeting hosted on another Zoom account it can only join On Behalf Of a signed-in user who is in that meeting. This page walks through adding it, using it and removing it, and shows where each requested scope is used.

What you need

  • No test credentials. Use any Zoom account. Sign-in is with Zoom itself, and access has been opened to every Zoom user for the review. (Once published it will be limited to pre-approved RHC Community member accounts.)
  • Two Zoom accounts, ideally: User A connects the app and brings the assistant into a meeting; User B joins as an ordinary participant, to see what everyone else sees. One account is enough for everything except step 6.
  • The app page: zoom.rhccommunity.com.

One meeting at a time. The assistant runs on a small server and can be in only one meeting at a time. If it's in use for a community meeting when you test, it can't join yours until that meeting ends. Occasionally it is used in ad-hoc meetings. This is unlikely, but if it happens, please try again later in the day. Most community meetings are around 1.5 hours, and are currently scheduled at:

  • Wednesday, 12 noon Vancouver time
  • Friday, 12 noon Vancouver time
  • Sunday, 10am Vancouver time
  • Sunday, 9pm Paris time

Scopes

  • user:read:user: read once at sign-in (step 1) to get the Zoom user ID. Only the ID is stored, to recognise the connection and act on deauthorization. Name and email are not stored.
  • user:read:token: used when User A asks the assistant to join (step 3), to request a one-meeting On Behalf Of token. The token is used at once and never stored.
  • user:read:zak and zoomapp:inmeeting: added automatically by the Meeting SDK feature and can't be removed. The app doesn't use them.

User A: add, use and remove the app

  1. Add (authorize). Open zoom.rhccommunity.com and press Sign in with Zoom. Zoom shows the permissions; press Allow. You're returned to the page, signed in, with a form to bring the assistant into a meeting.
  2. Start a meeting. With the same Zoom account, start a meeting (or join one hosted by someone else). Then, as host:
    • Let participants chat with everyone (in the chat panel, … → Participants can chat with → Everyone and anyone directly). This is needed for announcements and app replies.
    • Allow all participants to share their screen (Share Screen → Who can share? → All participants). This is needed in order to display slides.
  3. Add Meeting Assistant to the meeting. On the page, paste the meeting invite link, or enter the meeting ID and passcode, and press Join. The page shows its progress. If you aren't in the meeting yet, it says it's waiting for you, for up to 10 minutes. If the meeting has a waiting room, admit Meeting Assistant.
  4. Announcement. Shortly after it joins, the assistant posts a welcome message in the main chat.
  5. Chat commands. Send these in the chat, to everyone or directly to Meeting Assistant. It answers by direct message.
    • help: lists the commands you can use.
    • timer: shows a countdown in the assistant's video. The reply lists the controls: s start or stop, r to reset, a number such as 4 sets a timer for 4 minutes, and c closes the timer.
    • slides demo: the assistant shares a demonstration set of slides on screen. Again, the reply lists the controls: n for the next slide, b to go back, a number to jump to a specific slide, and s to stop. help lists other commands available while the slides are being displayed.
    • lock timer (host or co-host): only hosts and co-hosts can then use the timer. unlock timer opens it to everyone again.
  6. A second user. Have User B join the meeting and send help and timer: participants can operate the timer unless it's locked. User B sending leave now is refused: from the meeting chat, only hosts and co-hosts can have the assistant leave. (User A, who brought it in, can always have it leave from the web page, see step 7 below.)
  7. Have it leave. As User A (host), send leave. Meeting Assistant asks you to confirm, and leave now then makes it leave. Bring it in again (step 3) and press Ask it to leave on the page. It also leaves when the meeting ends, or if it is left alone in the meeting.
  8. Remove (deauthorize).
    • On the page, press Remove Meeting Assistant's access to my Zoom. The connection and tokens are immediately deleted, and tokens are revoked with Zoom.
    • Alternatively, sign in at marketplace.zoom.us, open Manage → Added Apps, and remove Meeting Assistant. When the app recieves Zoom's deauthorization notice it deletes the connection and tokens.

Data

Stored: the Zoom user ID and the OAuth tokens, encrypted. A join request (meeting number and status) is kept for 7 days; its passcode is deleted as soon as the assistant picks the request up. Audio, video and chat are never recorded or stored. See the privacy policy and technical architecture documentation submitted for the review.
